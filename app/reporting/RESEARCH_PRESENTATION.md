# Presenting coaching that players can understand and use

Research and design brief · 12 November 2026

## Decision

Present the existing report as a personal coaching document with this reading order:

**Player profile → contributions and insights, positives first → specific improvement priorities → supporting evidence and limits.**

Keep the established page width, vertical report, evidence/sequence pairings, practice module, offline packaging and data contract. Change the visual hierarchy and the default reading experience. The core report must communicate its value without opening a replay, selecting a tab, completing a checklist or writing a note. Existing tools remain optional.

The aim is earned recognition: the player recognises their situations, understands something they had not articulated, and knows which future decision to practise. Feeling recognised and improving play are separate outcomes. Presentation can support both; neither is established by making a report look premium.

## Scope and strength of the evidence

This is a targeted research synthesis across educational feedback, motivation, motor learning, goal-directed action, information presentation and accessibility. It is not a registered systematic review or a CS2 training experiment. Foundational work was combined with later reviews and contrary findings. Primary research, researchers' institutional copies, research syntheses and authoritative design/accessibility guidance were prioritised over coaching blogs and marketing claims.

The strongest transfer is about how to communicate evidence, distinguish observations from judgments, and make information readable. Transfer from classroom exercises, children's praise studies, medical note writing or physical motor tasks to adult competitive CS2 is indirect. The research does not establish an ideal number of insights, an optimal positive-to-negative ratio, an ideal report length or a measurable rank gain from this design.

Some publisher/full-text routes were unavailable. The source register distinguishes full-text material inspected from abstracts or accessible institutional summaries. Search-result dates were not treated as publication dates. The analysis snapshot was reused; none of these papers establishes what CanadianIQ actually did.

## 1. An insight must explain the play, not merely describe a result

Hattie and Timperley distinguish feedback about a task, its processes, self-regulation and the person. Their framework connects the learner's goal, current state and next step. For this product, the useful translation is to explain a decision mechanism and a future cue rather than attach a personal judgment to a statistic. This is an application of an educational framework, not a validated CS2 intervention. [Hattie & Timperley, 2007](https://assess.ucr.edu/sites/default/files/2019-02/hattietimperley_2007.pdf)

Use a stable insight grammar:

1. **What we observed:** a concise, specific statement with the relevant sample or round.
2. **What it means here:** the significance and a plausible mechanism, labelled appropriately.
3. **What to retain or change:** a decision the player can carry forward.
4. **Where it applies:** cue, game state and exceptions.
5. **What limits the interpretation:** the qualification that changes the advice.

Example: “The first kill gave your team 5v4. A different opponent killed you shortly afterward. Before the first exposure, consider which other approach could challenge it.” That offers a decision to examine. “Your awareness is poor” invents a diagnosis; “reduce your ten-second death rate” substitutes a proxy for the learning objective.

Shute's review supports feedback that supplies useful, specific information and attends to learner/task differences. Its discussion of explanations, hints and worked examples is particularly useful for a report that must teach through written situations. It does not support one universally optimal wording template. [Shute, 2007 research report](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)

## 2. Positives first should show what is worth retaining

A real positive tells the player which contribution already exists and deserves attention. It should have the same evidence standard as a correction. “You created 94 first advantages” is grounded in recorded enemy kills and count transitions. “You are a naturally gifted entry” adds unsupported role, ability and causality claims.

Positive-first is the requested editorial sequence, not a proven universal learning advantage. In two studies of medical students' written peer feedback, Parkes and colleagues found a mismatch between perceived benefit from a praise–critique–praise sandwich and measured performance evidence. This argues against using reassurance as proof of learning; it does not demonstrate that strengths should be omitted or that positive-first CS2 reports cannot work. [Parkes, Abercrombie & McCarty, 2013](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/feedback-sandwiches-affect-perceptions-but-not-performance/)

The large educational feedback meta-analysis by Wisniewski and colleagues found substantial variation by feedback characteristics, including information content. “More feedback” and “more praise” are therefore inadequate product specifications. This supports choosing useful information carefully rather than filling a quota of positives and negatives. [Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987456/)

For CanadianIQ, the Knowledge Expert recommended one clear positive: the 94 opening kills creating 5v4. The 37 round-end survivals remain context within that insight, not a second strength. Otherwise the presentation would quietly make survival the standard for judging the next action. Further positive modules should be added when their specific behaviours are supported; the page should not invent them to appear balanced.

## 3. Give the player an identity without locking them into a persona

Separate three kinds of identity:

| Layer | What belongs in it | What it must not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Personal identity | Recognisable handle, monogram, canonical player ID | An invented biography or imported pro persona |
| Current playing picture | Reviewed period, covered modules, observed contributions and contexts | A permanent trait or comprehensive diagnosis from one module |
| Development direction | A specific focus the player can practise | That they lack the ability or always make the same mistake |

Mueller and Dweck's experiments with fifth-graders found different responses to intelligence versus effort praise, including responses to setbacks. That is a useful warning about attaching value to fixed personal attributes. It is not direct evidence that a descriptive adult esports profile is harmful, and it is not a reason to replace tactical detail with generic praise for effort. [Mueller & Dweck, 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9686450/)

The profile therefore uses CanadianIQ's own name and verified ID, makes the sample recognisable, and states the module scope: opening fights in these games. It does not assign “aggressive entry,” “passive anchor,” rank, skill score or psychological type. A future role description needs role-sensitive evidence and room to change across maps, sides and team contexts. The existing project rejection of rigid personas remains in force.

## 4. Design for comprehension before interaction

Mayer and Moreno describe ways presentation can consume capacity needed for learning, including extraneous content, poorly signalled organisation and separated related explanations/graphics. Their research includes instructional multimedia and transfer tests, not coaching-report usability. The applicable design inference is to reduce unnecessary searching and put the explanation beside the evidence. The multimedia redundancy principle should not be misread as a ban on all useful repetition in static text. [Mayer & Moreno, 2003](https://cmapspublic2.ihmc.us/rid=1KXP7KR7M-8N27KG-1FNL/mayer_moreno_2003.pdf)

For this report:

- The entire conclusion, evidence summary and next-match question are visible in the default document.
- Every example includes a static player-count sequence beside its explanation. It conveys recorded states, not a fabricated line of sight, trajectory or safe route.
- Replays verify or enrich an explanation; they do not contain a missing essential paragraph.
- Notes and checklists support optional follow-up. They are not prerequisites for receiving coaching value.
- Detailed provenance stays in the existing source module. Material tactical limits remain beside their claims.

NN/g's eye-tracking guidance supports meaningful, visually distinct headings that help readers scan to relevant content. This is usability guidance rather than proof of learning or gameplay improvement. Use headings that state an observation, then body text that earns it. [Pernice, NN/g, 2019](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/)

## 5. Use visual evidence selectively

A chart is useful when it answers a real question more efficiently than prose. The existing timing distribution stays because it shows several possible follow-ups to an opening kill. Its caption explains the denominator and retrieval windows. The numbers are not an awareness score.

The static round sequences show an opening contribution and what followed. They must preserve the distinction between event order and tactical explanation. A player-count diagram cannot establish what the player knew, whether a second fight was forced, or whether a teammate could cover an angle.

Motor-feedback research is relevant but mixed in scope. Moinuddin and colleagues discuss visual, auditory and combined feedback, while noting restricted study selection in their review. That is not a basis for declaring video or animation always superior. The practical choice here is a readable explanation with an optional replay, rather than adding modalities merely to look sophisticated. [Moinuddin, Goel & Sethi, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8681883/)

## 6. Improvement priorities need a trigger and a flexible response

The action module should answer: when should I notice this, what should I consider doing, and how will I recognise progress? Keep a primary focus and a related follow-through rather than present a long defect list. The small number is an editorial choice for this report, not a scientifically established quota.

Gollwitzer and Sheeran describe linking a recognisable situation with a goal-directed response. Applied here, a cue can prompt a brief consideration of the next threat. A tactical implementation must stay flexible: it should not turn “after a kill” into an automatic retreat instruction or interrupt a necessary fight. [Gollwitzer & Sheeran, author chapter](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/08_Gollwitzer_Sheeran_Implementation_Intentions.pdf)

The sport goal-setting review examines goal types and their differing effects. It provides a sport-specific reason to distinguish process from performance/outcome goals, but it cannot prescribe this report's exact exercise. Use the observed behaviour as the review target, not a guaranteed performance gain. [Williamson and colleagues, systematic review/meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1080/1750984X.2022.2116723)

Current practice: before an opening exposure, consider where a second opponent could challenge and what cover or support could be used. After the kill, update when a meaningful choice exists. Progress means the next exposure fits the objective and information available. A lower quick-death count could instead reflect hesitation or missed necessary pressure, so it cannot serve as the sole success criterion.

## 7. Make uncertainty usable rather than burying it

The important distinction is between a reliable observed fact and an uncertain explanation. “A death occurred 1.44 seconds later” can be well supported while “the player had time to make a fresh choice” remains unproven. Use ordinary labels such as observed contribution, context to compare and practice focus. Avoid invented confidence percentages.

Van der Bles and colleagues found that communicating uncertainty did not produce the uniformly large loss of trust sometimes assumed, although effects depended on format and context. Their news/statistics experiments do not establish that every disclaimer improves trust in coaching. The design inference is narrower: there is no good basis for hiding a limitation merely to sound authoritative. [Van der Bles et al., 2020](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/117/14/7672.full.pdf)

Also avoid promising that positive identity styling increases motivation and therefore learning. Parma and colleagues found limited evidence for motivational mechanisms claimed in the OPTIMAL literature. Their review is a useful counterweight to turning an attractive theory into a product guarantee. [Parma, Miller & Bacelar, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38908415/)

## 8. Visual refresh within the existing layout

The visual direction is a readable coaching file: recognisable player masthead, restrained colour, quiet borders, consistent evidence labels and stronger typographic hierarchy. The existing vertical page and local two-column explanation/evidence relationship remain, with the requested reading order made explicit. There is no new dashboard, app shell or extraction pipeline.

| Existing module | Presentation change | Preserved contract |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Player identity, current picture and review scope | Canonical player and frozen sample |
| Insight/context | Positive contribution first, then outcome contrast and development signal | Same recorded events, denominator and limitations |
| Evidence | All four written examples and static sequences visible | Same round anchors; replay still available |
| Practice | Primary focus followed by conditional follow-through | Same bounded coaching theme and behaviour-based review |
| Source appendix | More readable typography; source inspector retained | Hashes, capabilities, exclusions and original warnings |
| Notes/replay tools | Explicitly optional secondary area | Existing notes, keys, exports and offline replay functionality |

Core text is larger than the prior micro-label-heavy version. Positive and development cues use words and symbols as well as colour. Light and dark themes use tested foreground/background pairs. WCAG specifies a 4.5:1 minimum for ordinary text and 3:1 for qualifying large text; passing selected colour checks is not a claim of complete accessibility conformance. [W3C, contrast minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)

## 9. What should be validated with players

Technical checks establish that the narrative is present, legible and functional. They cannot establish that the report feels insightful or changes decisions. A next user study should compare the previous and refreshed versions with similar content, control order effects, and ask the player to explain rather than merely rate attractiveness.

Useful tasks, requiring no interaction:

1. Describe the profile's scope and one evidenced contribution.
2. Explain why the Mirage win/loss pair matters.
3. State the primary practice cue in your own words.
4. Explain one limitation that prevents an overaggression verdict.
5. Apply the principle to a different round, including a situation where continued pressure is justified.

Measure what the player understood, what they misunderstood, unaided recall and transfer separately from feeling recognised, credibility and visual preference. Do not lead with the proposed answer or reward repeating a slogan. Subsequent gameplay review would need comparable contexts and information about the player's decisions; a simple before/after rate cannot establish causality.

This user study has not been run. The refresh is research-informed and technically checked, not experimentally proven to improve CS2 performance or perceived value.
