## Current coaching position

The CanadianIQ sample covers 50 games, seven maps and 1,134 eligible rounds. The player recorded 94 opening kills across 41 games and all seven maps. Those kills gave the team the first player-count advantage. Own death within ten seconds of an opening kill recurs in 25 rounds across 19 games and six maps. Twelve of those deaths came within two seconds across five maps. The recorded contribution supports the positive insight. The recurring transition supports practicing what can threaten the next exposure before committing to the opening fight. A repeated decision error would require comparable mechanisms, information the player could reasonably have, a feasible alternative and disconfirming cases across maps.

The report reads as profile, positive insights, then improvement priorities. Keep specific rounds, replay anchors and detailed tables in the optional Analyst Desk. Teach the player what situation to recognize, what to notice, and what choice to consider. Keep objective resetting as general guidance unless broader review supports an individual theme. Assign an identity only when representative evidence supports the trait. Use wins, losses, survival, forced fights and justified continuations to challenge the interpretation.

Post-plant advantage, last-alive and flash populations are ready for candidate selection. Use unique live plant events for post-plant eligibility. Keep eligible but unresolved cases in the population. Flash overlap is temporal evidence with separate counts for detonations, effects, links and source events. Preserve same-tick uncertainty and duplicate-event handling. Sampled movement, facing and approximate visibility have limited coverage. Knowledge, exact sightlines, trade feasibility, input execution and intent need separate evidence. Avoid inferring angle clearing, crosshair or movement quality from proxies alone.
