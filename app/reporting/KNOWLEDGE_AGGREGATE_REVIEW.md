### 2\. Minimum context before a player-facing lesson

**Q1 — Closing a post-plant advantage**

Before calling any sequence good or poor advantage management, establish the actual plant-time numbers, side, bomb phase, relevant time remaining, player positions, teammate coverage, subsequent engagements, and objective result.

Raw site IDs should remain raw identifiers until A/B mapping is verified. Planter coordinates must not be labeled exact C4 coordinates.

The strongest currently supported aggregate statement is\:

> Across the selected rounds, CanadianIQ was alive in 142 T-side post-plant situations where his team held a numerical advantage.

Whether he helped preserve or squander those advantages remains unestablished.

**Q2 — Last-alive decision-making**

For an individual lesson, establish the exact transition to last alive, opponents remaining, bomb status, available time, equipment where verified, and the subsequent action sequence.

A player facing multiple opponents may reasonably fight, reposition, seek an objective, or preserve equipment depending on the circumstances. Missing entry equipment and unverified visibility can make a supposed alternative impossible to defend.

The 21 episodes reaching 1v1 are useful retrieval candidates. Reaching 1v1 is not automatically correct play.

**Q3 — Flash coordination**

A personalized recommendation needs a complete sequence connecting the throw, affected opponent, teammate approach, recorded contact, and relevant timing.

The allowed claim is\:

> A teammate's recorded weapon damage or kill overlapped a blind interval attributed to CanadianIQ's flash against the same opponent.

That is more precise than “CanadianIQ enabled 172 teammate contacts.” The latter implies causal effectiveness the current data cannot establish.

Treat blind duration as a reported interval, not proof of continuous effective impairment. Without validated sightlines, enemy perception, or teammate intent, even apparently well-timed overlaps remain observational.

### 3\. Choosing positive and disconfirming examples

The report should not select examples by **win versus loss**\. Select them by the decision question and what the available evidence can actually discriminate.

For each candidate theme, include a positive example where the player made an identifiable, contextually useful choice, and actively search for a contrasting example that challenges the interpretation.

Examples of potentially useful contrasts\:

- **Q1\:** A post-plant round won despite poor apparent spacing, or lost despite reasonable support positioning.
- **Q2\:** A failed clutch attempt where the player made a defensible objective-aware decision, compared with a successful clutch where the same decision quality is uncertain.
- **Q3\:** A flash with overlapping teammate damage that appears incidental, compared with a flash without recorded damage that nevertheless coincided with a meaningful teammate entry or repositioning.

Those are **review targets**, not findings already established.

Avoid filtering to deaths, failed conversions, or successful flash contacts before searching for counterexamples. That would bias the coaching sample.

### 4\. The existing 3v2 comparison pair

**Yes—treat the same-game, same-map, same-raw-site, 3v2-at-plant win/loss pair as exploratory only.**

It is a useful candidate because several obvious variables are aligned. But it is not a controlled comparison.

Before using it to teach, inspect remaining time, equipment, actual player locations, defensive routes, utility, who held which responsibilities, and whether CanadianIQ had a meaningful alternative.

The pair may ultimately support a spatial or decision-sequence illustration. It may also demonstrate that different outcomes occurred despite similarly reasonable choices.

Do not preassign one round as correct and the other as mistaken.

Compare **new, non-overlapping games** against the prior session using the same opportunity definitions. If methodology or event coverage changes, either recompute comparable results or disclose the break.

An unchanged count can accompany better decisions. A better count can accompany worse decisions. Report the behavior first and the metric second.

## Editorial recommendation

I would advance the three questions to **targeted replay review**, prioritizing Q1 and Q2 for conceptually distinct coaching opportunities, with Q3 as a narrower coordination investigation.

Do not promote **closer**, **clutch player**, or **support player** identities yet.

The strongest next deliverable is a small set of annotated decision sequences answering\:

**What was the player's situation, what could reasonably be known, what options existed, what action occurred, and what should the player recognize next time?**

If those questions cannot be answered for a candidate, preserve it as an observation rather than manufacturing a coaching lesson.
