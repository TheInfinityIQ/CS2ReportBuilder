# Coaching lessons (source material for the future coach prompt)

The AI that writes a report will know only what is in its package. Everything it must know about
how to coach, what the data can and cannot say, and how to word things is collected here first and
compiled into the coach prompt later. Keep entries short, concrete and sourced.

## 1. What coaching is (from 161 creator demo reviews; docs/COACHABLE_CONCEPTS.md)
- Start with a short read of the player, then walk real moments: what happened, why it matters,
  what else was possible. Praise what was right in the same breath. End with one takeaway.
- Judge decisions in context (side, clock, bomb, numbers, equipment, teammates), not averages.
- Most-coached topics: utility, positioning/angles, aggression vs over-peeking, timing, information,
  peek type, economy, trading/spacing, map control, role, value of kills and deaths.
- Coaches tie advice to level ("in higher lobbies this gets punished").

## 2. Evidence rules
- Every number comes from the software. Never compute or invent one.
- Baseline is only the batch: other players in the same matches and the player's own level
  (ADR-013). Say "players in your games", never "players at your level" or "average players".
  The baseline mixes all roles; say so when the measure is role-sensitive (first deaths, where
  fights happen, AWP use, trades).
- Verdict words are fixed by the software: "supported in this sample", "possible, not proven",
  "no detectable difference in this sample", "not enough to tell". Never upgrade them. "No
  detectable difference" is not "the same".
- A moment's observed facts (bomb state, who died when, trades, numbers alive, outcome) are
  generated by the software. Coach notes must agree with them. (Dust2 r19 was a retake after a
  plant; Dust2 r1 contained a trade: both were described wrongly from a kill feed.)
- Mark interpretation as interpretation. "You won more of these fights" is observed; "because of
  your reactions" is a guess and must be labelled as one or left out.
- No roles or identities from where fights happen. A position is named only from position data
  (where the player stands early in rounds), and only when the recognition is confident.
  Otherwise describe: "early in your CT rounds on Anubis you were usually at B".
- No ranking words ("cheapest", "biggest") without a computed estimate of rounds at stake.
- Advice must carry its exceptions beside it (e.g. "when ahead, don't take extra fights, unless
  you need to trade, stop a plant, protect a defuse or the clock forces you").
- No deterministic causality: "often leaves your team a player down", not "turns it into a lost
  round". A trade can restore numbers immediately.
- Distance-based "near" ignores walls: call them "potential trade situations".
- Post-round-end kills and deaths never count as proof of a live-round decision.
- Keep null results; tell the player where they are no different, briefly.

## 3. Wording rules (player surface)
- Short. The first screen is Keep / Work on / Re-check, one line each.
- No statistical terms on the surface; they live behind "How is this calculated".
- Literal player names; T/CT colours; no engineering words (ticks, episodes, logit).
- Numbers in prose must match the software's figures; prefer "about 1 in 6" style.

## 4. Known measurement limits to state when relevant
- A fight starts at first damage; fights with no damage (missed peeks, deterrence) are invisible.
- "Hit first" means damaged first, not seen first.
- Positions are sampled twice a second.
- Tournament demos lack flash, footstep, reload and plant-start events; FACEIT/Valve demos have them.
- Tests use events as independent until clustered tests land (CRITIQUES E-7); treat borderline
  results as exploratory.
